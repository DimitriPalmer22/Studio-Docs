# Why Did I Even Start This Project?

I feel like I haven't been completely transparent with you guys about my motivations for starting this project. If I don't say it at some point, then it'll just keep eating at me until I do.

I feel like everyone on the team likes making games, or at least enjoys some part of the process. But, I want a little more than that.

### My Goal is to Have My Own Game Studio

> DISCLAIMER: This is not me trying to onboard you guys into signing up for a game studio or anything like that. I just want to be transparent about my goals. For me, this project is mainly for me to go through the growing pains of managing a large scale project and being responsible for multiple people.

Making games is pretty fun to me, especially when I feel like I can see the effects of my work. I like being able to look at something and say, "I made that".

After going through the Game Production class and feeling like I was barely able to contribute to our big project, I realized that I like having a clear role in a project. I like being able to see the fruits of my labor and feel like I'm actually making an impact.

### What is My Mission?

I don't just want to make games for the sake of making games. Yes, making games will always be the end goal, but I want to do it with a purpose.

I'm black. I've grown up in predominantly urban spaces my whole life. With that, I've been around a lot of people who don't have the same respect for the type of content we create or consume. My goal is to make games that are not only fun to play, but are also able to open up the minds of people who don't really understand the types of games I wanna make.

# "Sprint 2"

"Sprint 1" was for making the POC. It took a little longer than I expected, but it got done. Now, we're moving on to "Sprint 2". With sprint 1, we managed to create something that served as an example for what the game is supposed to play / look like.

Sprint 2 will actually be 2 weeks long. At the end of the sprint, if we feel like the game isn't where it's supposed to be, then it is what it is. We'll just adjust and keep working on the game in the next sprint.

The goal of Sprint 2 (and possibly sprint 3 as well) is to work toward something kinda like a vertical slice. I want as many of the core mechanics and concepts of the game to be fleshed out and implemented as possible. This way, in the later stages of development, we're just adding more of what we already have.

Specifically, I want to focus on the following things:
- Enemies (concepts, movement behavior, attacking behavior, etc.)
- Different spells for the player
- A rewarding upgrade system
- Fleshing out the existing rooms in the game (in terms of set-dressing and making the combat feel good within them)
- VFX implementation
- A basis for the game's music & SFX
- More clarification for the game's lore and world so that any narrative stuff that needs to be made can be done more easily

# Shop Room & Upgrade Room Discussion

> As of right now, most of this is ripped from [06-20 LD Meeting Notes](<./06-20 LD Meeting.md>), so if already read the shop & upgrade room section of that doc, then this portion of the document is redundant. Once we actually have the meeting, I'll update this with the things said in the meeting.

This is where I wanna discuss with the team more about the design of the shop room and upgrade room. I have some ideas, but I want to hear what you guys think.

This week, two designers are tasked with blocking out new room types: the shop room and the upgrade room. The concepts for these aren't completely fleshed out yet, so you guys will most likely have to converse with me more to fully get the idea out. I also plan to talk about this in our weekly group meeting.

#### Upgrade Room

Upgrade rooms are places where the player receives FREE upgrades to their tome.
- This should address the issue of the player's strength scaling with the difficulty of the game.

I currently have 2 ideas for how the upgrade room should be implemented. We'll run a poll or something in the weekly group meeting for what the upgrade room should be.

##### Idea 1: Another Person Inside Avernoth
- Inside the upgrade room, Allister finds another person.
- This person is also someone who has been sealed within Avernoth during the current war.
- This person has the special ability to upgrade your tome.
	- Remember, in the [initial notes for the game](<../Game Idea 1.md>), each tome remains relatively the same throughout its lifetime. One people with special skillsets and tools are able to upgrade a tome.
	- This person is one of those people.

This idea does raise a couple concerns:
- If the player can see this person in-game, then doesn't that mean we'd need ANOTHER character model for them?
- Also, we would probably need more dialogue for this person.
	- Why are they here?
	- How are they able to survive in Avernoth?
	- How do they manage to be found in the upgrade room(s) of every floor? Are they following Allister or are they just chilling there by coincidence?

##### Idea 2: Enchantment Table
- The upgrade room contains an enchantment table.
- Normally, Allister doesn't know how to use it, but one of his allies is able to walk him through the process.
	- This implies that there would be some type of dialogue portal or something that the player can interact with to upgrade their tome.
- This enchantment table would need a fairly distinct look to it, so that players can easily recognize it as the upgrade room.

#### Shop Room

The shop is a place where the player can purchase items to help them on their journey. The items the player gets in the shop are distinct from the upgrades they get within the upgrade room.

##### Possible Items to Purchase
- Consumables (like how in Enter The Gungon, you can buy keys or blanks)
- Other Tomes (if you are displeased with the tome you have, you can purchase a new one)
- Upgrades for the player's max health
- Upgrades for the player's max mana
- Pickup that immediately restore the player's health
- Pickup that immediately increases one of the player's base stats

##### Why is there a "Shop Room" in Avernoth?

- It doesn't make sense for there to be a traditional shop in Avernoth with a traditional currency (like gold or money).
- Is there a shopkeeper in the shop? What is stopping Allister from just taking the items?

##### Gameplay Considerations for the Shop
- Allowing the player to get items too easily within the shop dampens the difficulty of the game.
- So, we should
	- Make items pretty expensive
	- Don't allow items to infinitely restock

### Currency
- It doesn't make sense for the currency to be gold or money.
- We know that, like most rogue-likes, the player will get currency whenever they defeat enemies / clear their current room.
- So, we should probably focus on the enemies themselves to theme the currency.
	- Kinda like how in Dark Souls, the currency is souls, which you get from defeating enemies.
- In our game, we can play into the whole magic energy thing.
	- Whenever the player defeats an enemy, the enemy drops a couple of magical orbs that Allister absorbs.
	- Like when you kill enemies in [Hollow Knight](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/eau5vc-uRk0)
- We need to workshop this more.
	- What do these orbs look like?
	- What do they represent?
	- What are they called?
	- How exactly is it used as a currency? Is it just exchanged? Is it put into a vending machine??? Is it used as some type of special tool?
- We'll talk about this more in the weekly group meeting.

#### Extra Idea for Currency
- As of right now, the upgrade rooms are completely free, and the items in the shop are not the most compelling thing in the world to spend money on.
- Also, since our game is completely linear, the player can't really go back to a shop or upgrade room once they leave.
- We can use these rooms as gates to reward the player for good gameplay.
	- Oh, you didn't have enough money by the time you entered the shop? You shoulda played better.

- So, what if we gave the player another opportunity to spend their currency?
- What if, in the upgrade room, the player could CHOOSE to spend money to increase the potency of their upgrade?
	- The upgrade itself is still free, but on the upgrade screen, the player can choose to spend some currency to increase the potency of the upgrade.
- This introduces a risk-reward system where the player can choose to spend their currency to make their upgrade better, but at the cost of not being able to use that currency for other items in the shop.
- Is this a fun and engaging mechanic?
